What does this Passport Size Photo Maker do?

This free online passport photo maker creates compliant passport, visa, and ID photos for 20+ countries — entirely in your browser. Choose your country, upload a selfie or portrait, and the tool crops, resizes, and prepares your photo to the exact official dimensions in millimetres, centimetres, inches, or pixels at any DPI you choose.

Unlike tools that upload your face to a server, everything here runs locally using HTML5 Canvas and JavaScript. Your photo never leaves your device — no upload, no server processing, no logs. The tool provides:

  • Exact country dimensions — pull-down with US, UK, EU/Schengen, India, Canada, Australia, China, Japan, Germany, France, Spain, South Korea, Brazil, and more, each pre-configured to official specifications.
  • Custom size in any unit — enter any width and height in mm, cm, inches, or pixels. DPI can be set to 72, 150, 200, 300, or 600 for perfect pixel↔physical conversion.
  • Head position guide — an oval overlay showing where your head should sit, with green and red lines marking the head top and chin positions for each country's rules.
  • Real face auto-detection — an "Auto-fit" button uses MediaPipe's face detector to find your face and automatically position the crop. Works fully client-side.
  • Background cleanup — a slider that brightens light backgrounds toward pure white, plus solid-colour options for white, light grey, cream, and light blue.
  • Dark print mode — a toggle that slightly darkens the photo so it looks correct on paper instead of washed out.
  • KB targeting — compress the output to a specific file size (20 KB, 50 KB, 100 KB, 200 KB, or any custom number) for online portals with strict upload limits.
  • Multiple download formats — JPEG at any quality, PNG for lossless output, and multi-copy print sheets at 4×6, 5×7, A4, or Letter.

How to make a passport photo

  1. Upload your photo: Drag and drop a clear, well-lit portrait into the upload zone. Front-facing, neutral expression, no glasses, plain background works best.
  2. Choose your country or custom size: Pick from the dropdown. For custom sizes, enter width and height and choose the unit (mm, cm, in, px). Set DPI if converting between units.
  3. Position your face: Drag the photo to reposition. Use the zoom buttons to resize. The green line should sit just above the top of your head, and the red line should sit at your chin. Optionally click Auto-fit to let face detection position it for you.
  4. Tune appearance: Adjust Brightness, Contrast, and Saturation with the sliders. Use the Cleanup slider to brighten a light background toward white. Enable Dark print mode if the printed result looks too light.
  5. Download: Click Download Photo for a JPEG, Download PNG for a lossless file, or Compress to KB to hit a specific file size. Use Download Print Sheet to get a multi-copy sheet ready for printing.

How to take a compliant passport photo at home

Getting the photo right is 80% of the job. Most rejections aren't about size — they're about lighting, expression, or the background. Follow these tips before uploading:

Lighting

  • Face a window during the day. Soft, even daylight is the best light source for passport photos. Avoid direct sunlight which creates harsh shadows.
  • Avoid overhead lights. Ceiling bulbs cast shadows under the eyes and nose, which can be flagged as "shadow on face."
  • No flash. Camera flash flattens features and can cause red-eye. Turn it off.
  • Even lighting on both sides. If one side of your face is noticeably darker, the photo may be rejected.

Background

  • Stand 50–60 cm in front of a plain wall. A white, off-white, or light grey wall is ideal for most countries.
  • Avoid patterns, doors, or furniture. The background must be uniform — no visible objects, shadows, or texture.
  • No shadows on the wall. If your body casts a shadow, move forward or ask someone to hold a sheet behind you.

Expression and pose

  • Neutral expression. Mouth closed, eyes open, no smiling. Some countries (US, UK, Canada) strictly require a neutral face.
  • Look straight at the camera. Head centred, both eyes visible, no tilting.
  • Remove glasses. Most countries now prohibit glasses in passport photos, even prescription ones.
  • No hats or head coverings unless worn for religious reasons. Even then, the full face must be visible from the bottom of the chin to the top of the forehead.
  • Hair off the face. Both ears don't need to be visible in every country, but your full face from forehead to chin must be clear.

Tips for printing at home

  • Print at 100% scale. In your printer dialog, choose "Actual size" or "100%" — never "Fit to page" or "Shrink to fit". Scaling will change the physical dimensions and the photo will be rejected.
  • Use photo paper, not plain paper. Matte or glossy photo paper gives the sharpest, most professional result. Plain paper absorbs ink and looks washed out.
  • Print at 300 DPI or higher. The generated print sheet is already encoded at 300 DPI. Make sure your printer is set to at least "High" or "Photo" quality.
  • Use Dark print mode if needed. Screens display colours more vibrantly than paper reflects them, so a photo that looks perfect on screen may look washed out when printed. Enable Dark print mode in the adjustments panel.
  • Cut on the dashed lines. Use a paper trimmer or sharp scissors. Aim for the middle of the dashed cut guide so the photo keeps a small white border.
  • Let the ink dry. Give the print 2–3 minutes to dry before handling, especially on glossy paper. Wet ink smudges.
  • Check the size with a ruler. After cutting, measure the photo. It should match the country's required dimensions exactly (for example 35×45 mm for the UK).
  • Photo labs are an option. If you don't have a photo printer, take the downloaded single photo to a pharmacy, photo shop, or print kiosk and ask for "exact size" printing.
