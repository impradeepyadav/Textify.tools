What does this Keyword Density Checker do?

This free online keyword density checker analyzes any text and shows you exactly how often each word and phrase appears. It breaks down the results into 1-word, 2-word, and 3-word phrases — so you can see not just individual keywords, but the multi-word phrases your content leans on most.

Keyword density is the percentage of times a keyword appears in your content compared to the total word count. The formula is simple: (Keyword Count / Total Words) × 100. For example, if your 1,000-word article contains your target keyword 15 times, the density is 1.5%.

While keyword density is no longer a direct ranking factor in modern SEO, it's still a useful signal for detecting over-optimization and understanding what your content is about. This tool helps you spot keyword stuffing before Google does, and gives you a clear picture of your content's focus.

How to check keyword density

  1. Paste your content into the input box. You can paste a full article, a blog draft, a landing page, or any block of text.
  2. Adjust the options if needed. You can exclude stop words (common words like "the" and "and"), enable case sensitivity, or filter out short words.
  3. Click Analyze. The tool instantly processes your text and displays total words, unique words, and character count.
  4. Switch between tabs to view 1-word, 2-word, and 3-word phrase analysis. Each tab shows the phrase, how many times it appeared, and its density percentage.
  5. Check the color coding. Green means optimal density (0.5–2%), yellow means borderline (2–3%), and red means keyword stuffing risk (over 3%).
  6. Copy or download the results for your SEO report or client deliverable.

Keyword density formula

The calculation is straightforward. For any keyword or phrase, divide the number of times it appears by the total word count, then multiply by 100.

Density = (Keyword Count / Total Words) × 100

Example: You've written a 1,000-word blog post and your focus keyword "keyword density checker" appears 15 times. The density is (15 / 1,000) × 100 = 1.5%.

This tool applies the same formula to every 1-word, 2-word, and 3-word phrase it finds in your text. Multi-word phrases are especially useful because they reveal what your content is really about — a page about "keyword density" will naturally have that phrase appear frequently, and the 2-word and 3-word analysis will confirm it.

What's the ideal keyword density?

Most SEO professionals recommend a primary keyword density of 1% to 2%. Below 0.5% is under-optimized, and above 3% risks being flagged as keyword stuffing.

DensityRatingWhat it means
0.5% – 2%✅ OptimalNatural, readable, and well-optimized for the keyword.
2% – 3%⚠️ BorderlineGetting close to over-optimization. Consider varying your phrasing.
Above 3%🛑 Stuffing riskKeyword stuffing detected. Reduce repetitions and use synonyms.
Below 0.5%⚠️ Under-optimizedThe keyword is barely present. Add it naturally in strategic places.

That said, the "ideal" density varies by keyword and niche. Some competitive terms naturally have higher densities on top-ranking pages; others do fine at 0.8%. The more important question is whether your content reads naturally. If you're forcing a keyword into every other sentence, it's too much — regardless of what the percentage says.

Keyword density vs. modern SEO: what actually matters

Keyword density is a useful diagnostic, but it's not the whole picture. Modern search engines like Google care far more about topical relevance and natural language than about hitting a specific density percentage. Here's what matters more in 2026:

1. Keyword placement

Where your keyword appears matters more than how often. The highest-impact placements are:

  • Title tag / H1 — required
  • First 100 words — required
  • At least one H2 subheading — recommended
  • Conclusion — recommended
  • Naturally distributed through the body — natural

2. Related keywords and semantic terms

Instead of repeating the same phrase, use synonyms and semantically related terms. Google's language models understand that "keyword density checker," "keyword density tool," and "SEO keyword analysis" are all related. Using variations signals topical depth without triggering stuffing warnings.

3. Readability

If you're writing for a reader, the density naturally falls into the right range. If you're writing for a percentage, the reader can tell — and so can Google. Aim for a Flesch Reading Ease score of 60–70 and keep sentences under 20 words on average.

4. Topical authority

One page with perfect keyword density won't outrank a site that covers a topic comprehensively. It's better to have 10 related articles that each target a different angle than to stuff 50 mentions of the same keyword into one page.
