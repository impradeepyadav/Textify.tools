What is a shipping label generator?

A shipping label generator is a tool that produces the printed label you attach to a parcel before sending it through a courier, postal service, or freight company. The label typically contains two addresses (sender and recipient), a tracking number, weight and package dimensions, and often a barcode for automated scanning during transit.

This tool creates a clean, courier-ready label directly in your browser. You don't need to install any software, create an account, or upload your shipping details to a third-party server. Everything stays on your device — which is especially important when you're handling customer data for a small business.

Built for global shippers

Shipping isn't the same everywhere. A label that works for a US warehouse shipping domestic FedEx doesn't automatically work for a seller in Germany shipping to France, or an Etsy seller in India shipping to the UK. This tool adapts to your country, currency, and unit preferences.

  • Country selector — shows format hints for ZIP/postal codes and phone numbers for 30+ countries.
  • Multi-currency — 20+ currencies with correct symbols for declared value, COD, and item prices.
  • Metric or imperial — switch between kg/cm and lb/in across the whole tool.
  • Carrier presets — UPS, FedEx, DHL, USPS, Royal Mail, Australia Post, Canada Post, India Post, Japan Post.
  • HS codes & contents — customs-ready fields for international shipments.
  • Incoterms — DDP, DAP, EXW, FOB, CIF for B2B international trade.
  • Country of origin — required by many customs authorities.
  • Multi-language label — labels available in 10 languages including English, Spanish, French, German, Hindi, Arabic, Chinese, and Japanese.

How to use the shipping label generator

  1. Pick your country, currency, and unit system at the top.
  2. Upload a logo (optional) and pick an accent color.
  3. Enter the sender details. Name or business, full address, phone, and optional email.
  4. Enter the recipient details. Double-check the ZIP or postal code.
  5. Fill in package details — weight, dimensions, pieces, reference, declared value, HS code, and contents.
  6. Add a COD amount if applicable.
  7. Set a tracking number or use a carrier preset to auto-generate one.
  8. Pick a barcode format and QR code content.
  9. Enable handling marks for safe transit.
  10. Download PDF, print, or add more recipients in Batch Mode.

Tips for printing a professional label

1. Use a laser or thermal printer

Inkjet prints can smudge when exposed to rain or rough handling. Laser and thermal printers produce crisp, water-resistant results.

2. Print at 100% scale

Never use "fit to page" — the barcode must print at the correct physical size to scan properly.

3. Match the paper size

Use A4 in most countries, US Letter in North America, and 4×6 thermal for high-volume sellers.

4. Check customs paperwork

For international shipments, always include HS codes, contents, and country of origin. Missing details lead to customs delays.

5. Cover the whole label

Never tape over just part of the label. Water can seep under the edges and damage the barcode.

6. Keep a copy

Save a PDF of every label. If something goes wrong, you have proof of what was sent.
