What does this Week Number Calculator do?

This free online week number calculator tells you the week number for any date, in both the ISO 8601 standard used across most of the world and the US week numbering system used in the United States and a few other countries. It also works in reverse: enter a year and week number, and it shows you the exact start and end dates of that week.

Week numbers are used everywhere — from project planning and payroll to school schedules and ISO-formatted business reports. But because different countries use different conventions for when a week starts and which week is "week 1," the same date can have different week numbers depending on where you are. This calculator shows both systems side by side, so there's no confusion.

Everything runs locally in your browser. No server, no signup, no tracking. Your dates never leave your device.

How ISO 8601 week numbers work

ISO 8601 is the international standard for date and time representation. Its week-numbering rules are used by most countries in Europe, Asia, and Oceania, as well as by many international organisations and software systems.

The three rules

  1. Weeks start on Monday and end on Sunday.
  2. Week 1 is the week that contains the first Thursday of January — equivalently, the week that contains January 4th.
  3. A year has 52 or 53 weeks. The 53rd week appears when January 1 falls on a Thursday (common year) or on a Wednesday (leap year).

Because of the Thursday rule, the first few days of January sometimes belong to week 52 or 53 of the previous year, and the last few days of December sometimes belong to week 1 of the next year. This is what makes ISO week numbers occasionally counter-intuitive — but it keeps weeks aligned with the calendar year in a way that US week numbers don't.

ISO vs US week numbers: what's the difference?

The biggest difference between the two systems is which day a week starts on and which week counts as week 1.

Feature ISO 8601 US (WEEKNUM)
Week starts onMondaySunday
Week ends onSundaySaturday
Week 1 containsFirst Thursday of January (≈ Jan 4)January 1
Range of week numbers1–52 or 1–531–53 or 1–54
Used byEurope, Asia, Oceania, ISO-standard reportsUnited States, Canada, some Middle Eastern countries

Because of these differences, the same date can have different week numbers in the two systems. For example, December 31, 2025, is week 1 of 2026 under ISO 8601 (because it falls in the same week as Jan 1, 2026, which is a Thursday), but it is week 53 of 2025 under the US system.

Why some years have 53 weeks

Most years have 52 ISO weeks, but some have 53. A year has 53 weeks when one of these is true:

  • It is a common year (365 days) and January 1 falls on a Thursday.
  • It is a leap year (366 days) and January 1 falls on a Wednesday.

This happens because 365 days is exactly 52 weeks plus 1 day, and 366 days is 52 weeks plus 2 days. Under the ISO Thursday rule, that extra day (or two) can push the year into a 53rd week when it lands at the right spot in the week.

The pattern of 53-week years repeats roughly every 5–6 years, but the exact spacing depends on leap year placement. Well-known 53-week years include 2020, 2026, 2032, 2037, and 2043.
