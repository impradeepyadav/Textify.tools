What does this income tax calculator do?

How income tax works

Most countries use a progressive tax system, where your income is divided into brackets and each bracket is taxed at a progressively higher rate. This means the more you earn, the higher the percentage of tax you pay on the last portion of your income.

Key terms

  • Gross income: Your total income before any taxes or deductions.
  • Standard deduction / Personal allowance: A fixed amount that reduces your taxable income before tax is applied. Most countries provide this automatically.
  • Taxable income: Gross income minus the standard deduction or personal allowance.
  • Marginal tax rate: The rate applied to your last dollar of income — the highest bracket you fall into.
  • Effective tax rate: Your total tax divided by your gross income, expressed as a percentage. It's always lower than your marginal rate.

How the calculation works

For example, in the US, a single filer earning $75,000 in 2025 would subtract the $15,000 standard deduction, leaving $60,000 in taxable income. That $60,000 is then taxed in layers:

  • The first $11,925 is taxed at 10% = $1,192.50
  • The next $36,550 is taxed at 12% = $4,386.00
  • The remaining $11,525 is taxed at 22% = $2,535.50
  • Total tax = $8,114.00
  • Net income = $75,000 − $8,114 = $66,886
  • Effective rate = $8,114 / $75,000 = 10.8%
  • Marginal rate = 22%

This is why your effective tax rate is always lower than your marginal rate — only the income in the top bracket is taxed at the highest rate.

Country-specific notes

  • United States: Federal tax only. State taxes, Social Security, and Medicare are not included. Standard deduction for 2025: $15,000 single, $30,000 married filing jointly.
  • United Kingdom: Income tax only. National Insurance contributions are not included. Personal allowance of £12,570, which phases out above £100,000.
  • India: New tax regime for FY 2025-26. Standard deduction of ₹75,000 for salaried taxpayers. Section 87A rebate makes income up to ₹12 lakh effectively tax-free.
  • Canada: Federal tax only. Provincial taxes are not included. Basic Personal Amount of $16,129 for income under $177,882.
  • Australia: Income tax only. Medicare levy of 2% is not included. Tax-free threshold of $18,200.
  • Germany: Progressive tax starting at 14% and rising to 45%. Basic tax-free allowance of €12,096 for singles. Church tax and solidarity surcharge not included.
  • France: Progressive tax from 0% to 45%. Family quotient (parts) system applies — this calculator uses a single part.
  • Japan: National income tax only. Local inhabitant tax of 10% and reconstruction surtax of 2.1% are not included. Basic deduction varies by income.
