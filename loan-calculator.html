What this loan calculator does

This tool takes three inputs — the loan amount, the annual interest rate, and the loan term — and instantly calculates your monthly payment, total interest paid, and total cost of borrowing. It works for any type of loan: car, personal, student, business, or mortgage. Because it runs entirely in your browser, no data is ever sent to a server.

Unlike a simple payment estimate, this calculator also shows a year-by-year amortization table. That reveals how the mix of principal and interest shifts over time. In the early years, most of your payment goes to interest; in later years, more goes to principal. Understanding this pattern is essential if you're considering prepayment or refinancing.

The loan payment formula

Every fixed-rate loan uses the same amortization formula:

Monthly Payment = [P × R × (1 + R)N] ÷ [(1 + R)N − 1]
  • P — Loan principal (the amount borrowed).
  • R — Monthly interest rate. If the annual rate is 6.5%, then R = 6.5 ÷ 12 ÷ 100 = 0.005417.
  • N — Total number of monthly payments. A 5-year loan has N = 60.

Because interest is applied to the outstanding balance each month, not the original amount, the cost of borrowing is always higher than principal × rate × years. The amortization table above shows exactly how this plays out.

Understanding loan types

Different loans carry different risk for the lender, and that translates into different interest rates and terms. Here's a quick comparison:

Loan TypeTypical RateTypical TermCommon Use
Auto loan4% – 12%3 – 7 yearsBuying a car, secured by the vehicle
Personal loan6% – 20%1 – 5 yearsDebt consolidation, emergencies, big purchases
Student loan4% – 10%5 – 20 yearsTuition, living expenses during study
Business loan7% – 18%1 – 10 yearsExpansion, equipment, working capital
Home mortgage3% – 8%15 – 30 yearsBuying a home, secured by the property

Secured loans (auto, mortgage) typically have lower rates because the lender can recover assets if you default. Unsecured loans (personal, student) carry higher rates because the lender takes on more risk.

How loan term affects what you pay

This is the single most misunderstood part of borrowing. A longer term lowers your monthly payment — but dramatically increases the total interest. Here's what a $25,000 auto loan at 6.5% costs at different terms:

TermMonthly PaymentTotal InterestTotal Cost
3 years$766$2,582$27,582
5 years$489$4,349$29,349
7 years$371$6,160$31,160

Doubling the term from 3 to 7 years drops the monthly payment by nearly 52% — but nearly doubles the total interest. If you can afford the shorter term without stretching your budget, it almost always saves money.

How to get the best loan terms

1. Improve your credit score first

Raising your score from 650 to 750 can shave 1–2% off the interest rate — worth thousands over the life of a loan.

2. Compare multiple lenders

Rates vary widely. Getting at least three quotes lets you negotiate and often saves more than 1%.

3. Make a larger down payment

Every dollar down reduces the principal — and the interest that compounds on it.

4. Choose the shortest affordable term

A shorter term means higher monthly payments but far less total interest.

5. Ask about fees

Origination fees, processing charges, and insurance add-ons can quietly raise your total cost by 1–3%.

6. Keep debt-to-income low

Lenders offer better rates when your existing debt isn't already high.

Prepayment — the fastest way to save

Any extra payment you make goes directly toward the principal. That reduces the balance on which future interest is calculated — so the savings compound over time.

Take a $25,000 auto loan at 6.5% for 5 years. Your monthly payment is about $489 and you'll pay $4,349 in interest. If you add just $50 per month toward principal, you'll:

  • Pay off the loan in roughly 4 years and 3 months instead of 5 years.
  • Save approximately $580 in interest.
  • Own the car free and clear nine months earlier.

Always check whether your lender charges a prepayment penalty. Many do not, but some do in the first 1–3 years.

Common loan mistakes to avoid

  • Borrowing more than you need. Lenders may approve more than is comfortable. Borrow only what fits your budget.
  • Focusing only on the monthly payment. A low payment over a long term can mean paying thousands in extra interest.
  • Skipping the amortization schedule. Understanding how principal and interest shift over time helps you plan prepayments effectively.
  • Ignoring the total cost. Always add interest plus fees when comparing loan offers.
  • Signing without reading. Prepayment penalties, variable-rate clauses, and mandatory insurance can be buried in fine print.

Glossary of loan terms

TermMeaning
PrincipalThe original amount borrowed, before interest.
AmortizationThe process of paying off a loan through scheduled payments of principal and interest.
APRAnnual Percentage Rate — the total yearly cost of a loan including interest and fees.
TermThe length of the loan, usually in months or years.
PrepaymentPaying extra toward the principal, reducing future interest.
RefinancingReplacing an existing loan with a new one, often to get a lower rate.
Secured loanA loan backed by an asset (car, house) that the lender can seize on default.
Unsecured loanA loan not backed by collateral — usually carries a higher rate.
