Why Use This Word Counter?

I needed a word counter that didn't force me to sign up, didn't feel sluggish, and didn't send my text to some server in the cloud. So I built one that ticks all three boxes — and then added a few things I always wished other counters had, like readability scores and local history. Here's what you get:

Instant, live stats

No need to click a button. Every number updates as you type or paste.

Complete privacy

Your text stays on your device. I don't see it, and I don't store it.

Readability scores

Flesch Reading Ease and Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level, shown as clear numbers.

Text history

Save multiple versions locally and compare them anytime (stored in your browser).

Works offline

Once the page loads, you can disconnect and keep working.

Multi-language support

Character counting works for any language. Word counting works for any space-separated language.

How to Use the Word Counter

  1. Paste or type your text into the box above.
  2. Watch the stats appear instantly in the sidebar — words, characters, sentences, and more.
  3. Use the toolbar inside the textarea to clear, copy, change case, or export your text.

That's it. No signup, no pop-ups, just a clean, powerful word counter.

How People Actually Use It (Typical Scenarios)

Polishing a college essay

A student pastes their 1,500-word draft, sees that the Flesch-Kincaid grade level is a bit high for their audience, and simplifies a few sentences to bring the readability down. The final version lands right in the sweet spot.

Checking meta description length

An SEO specialist pastes a meta description into the counter, instantly sees the character count (with spaces), and trims it to fit Google's recommended length. The live character-with-spaces number makes it effortless.

Trimming a Twitter thread

A social media manager extracts all hashtags from their thread, discovers they've used 12, and cuts them down to the 5 most relevant ones. The extract panel saves the day.

Debugging application logs

A developer pastes a long error log into the counter and uses the character breakdown to make sure the log contains only ASCII letters and digits. The detailed stats make debugging a little less painful.

Tips to Make the Most of This Word Counter

  • Use the history to save multiple drafts. It's like a tiny version control system for your writing.
  • Adjust the speaking WPM to get a realistic presentation time. 130–150 WPM is a natural pace for most speakers.
  • Keep an eye on lexical density — if it's very low, your vocabulary may be repetitive. A score of 50–70% is often a sign of varied, engaging text.
  • Export as PDF or DOCX when you need a formatted copy for sharing or printing.
