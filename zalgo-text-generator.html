What Is Zalgo Text?

Zalgo text is ordinary text that has been overloaded with Unicode combining marks — tiny diacritical symbols that stack above, through, and below each letter. Instead of sitting neatly on the baseline, the characters appear to bleed, melt, or vibrate. The result looks corrupted, haunted, or glitched, which is exactly why it became popular in horror-themed posts, creepypasta threads, meme captions, and glitch-art typography.

People search for it under many names: creepy text generator, glitch text generator, corrupted text generator, scary text generator, or simply Zalgo text maker. Whatever you call it, the underlying technique is the same — real Unicode characters layered on top of your original letters. Nothing is drawn, filtered, or faked with images.

Customize Your Zalgo Text

The generator gives you fine control over how corrupted the output looks:

  • Craziness Level — from 1 to 100. Low values add a faint shimmer; high values produce a dense wall of stacked marks.
  • Marks per Character — sets the maximum number of combining marks added at 100% craziness. Lower it if you want the text to stay readable.
  • Direction toggles — Up places marks above letters, Middle places overlay marks through them, and Down places marks below. Enabling all three gives the classic Zalgo appearance.
  • Presets — Subtle Glitch, Light Zalgo, Classic Zalgo, Creepy, Chaotic, and Extreme Zalgo cover the most common looks in a single click.
  • Random variation — makes the number of marks differ slightly from character to character, which looks more organic than a perfectly even pattern.
  • Randomize Effect — reshuffles which marks are used without changing your original text at all.

What Are Unicode Combining Marks?

Unicode doesn't just contain letters — it also contains combining marks, a category of characters designed to attach to the character before them. A base letter such as e followed by a combining acute accent U+0301 renders as é.

Because these marks attach rather than stand alone, you can stack many of them on a single base character. The renderer tries to position each one above, through, or below the letter, and once the stack gets tall enough the glyph looks broken and glitchy. That stacking behaviour is what powers every Unicode glitch text effect, including this generator.

This tool uses a curated set of combining diacritical marks rather than every code point in the combining ranges. That keeps the output valid Unicode, keeps it copyable, and avoids the blank boxes and rendering glitches that come from using marks that many fonts don't support.
