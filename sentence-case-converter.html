What does this Sentence Case Converter do?

This free online sentence case converter takes any text and converts it to proper sentence case. In sentence case, only the first letter of the first word in each sentence (or heading) is capitalized, along with any proper nouns. Everything else is lowercase. This is the standard capitalization style used in normal prose writing, blog posts, articles, and website copy.

Unlike a simple "lowercase everything except the first letter" tool, this converter intelligently handles multiple sentences, preserves proper nouns (days of the week, months, countries, major cities, common names, and well-known brands), and keeps acronyms like NASA, HTML, and URL in uppercase.

Everything runs locally in your browser. Your text never leaves your device, there is no signup, and there are no limits on how much you can convert.

Sentence case vs. title case

Sentence case and title case are the two most common capitalization styles for headings and titles. Here is how they differ:

StyleRuleExampleBest for
Sentence caseCapitalize first word and proper nouns only"The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog"Body text, blog posts, articles, website copy, APA titles
Title caseCapitalize major words; minor words stay lowercase"The Quick Brown Fox Jumps Over the Lazy Dog"Headlines, book titles, formal titles, Chicago/AP headlines

Sentence case is generally easier to read for long passages and is the preferred style for most modern web content, including Google's developer documentation and many major style guides like APA.

Sentence case rules explained

Capitalize these words

  • The first word of each sentence: Always capitalized, no exceptions.
  • Proper nouns: Names of people (John, Mary), places (Paris, London), organizations (Google, Microsoft), nationalities (American, French), days (Monday), months (January), and brands (iPhone, Coca-Cola).
  • The pronoun "I": Always capitalized, even in the middle of a sentence.
  • Acronyms: NASA, HTML, URL, API, and similar abbreviations stay in uppercase.
  • First word after a colon: Following APA Style, the first word after a colon in a heading or title is capitalized.[reference:0]

Lowercase these words

  • All common nouns, verbs, adjectives, and adverbs that are not proper nouns.
  • Articles (a, an, the), prepositions (of, in, to, at), and conjunctions (and, but, or) unless they begin a sentence.

Special cases

  • Multi-sentence text: Each sentence is treated independently. After a period, exclamation mark, or question mark, the next word is capitalized.
  • Colons: The first word after a colon is capitalized when it introduces a subtitle or heading, following APA Style guidelines.[reference:1]
  • Acronyms vs. regular words: Words that are all-uppercase in the input (2+ letters) are treated as acronyms and preserved.
