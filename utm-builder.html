What is a UTM builder?


A UTM builder is a tool that appends campaign tracking parameters — the five utm_ tags recognised by Google Analytics and most other analytics platforms — to a destination URL. When someone clicks that tagged link, the analytics platform records exactly which campaign, channel, and creative brought them in. Without UTM parameters, most analytics tools cannot tell the difference between a visitor from a Google ad, a Facebook post, or a newsletter.


The three required parameters are utm_source, utm_medium, and utm_campaign. The optional utm_term identifies a paid search keyword, and utm_content distinguishes between different ads or A/B variants in the same campaign. Some platforms also use utm_id for campaign identifiers.


Every URL built here is generated entirely in your browser. Nothing is sent to a server, nothing is stored remotely, and the tool keeps working after you go offline.

UTM parameters explained


Each UTM parameter captures a different dimension of your campaign. The three required tags are enough for most reporting; the optional ones add depth when you need it.


ParameterRequiredWhat it identifiesExamples


utm_sourceYesThe platform or site sending the trafficgoogle, facebook, newsletter


utm_mediumYesThe channel or marketing mediumcpc, social, email, banner

Best practices for UTM tags


    

  • Use lowercase everywhere. Analytics platforms treat Google and google as two different sources. Lowercase everything to keep reporting clean.
    • 

  • Use underscores or hyphens instead of spaces. Spaces get encoded as %20, which makes the URL harder to read and can break some analytics tools.
    • 

  • Be consistent. Pick a naming convention (e.g. utm_source=facebook always, never fb or Facebook) and stick to it across all campaigns.
    • 

  • Never put UTM parameters on internal links. Doing so resets the session's original attribution in most analytics tools, so the visit gets credited to the internal link instead of the real acquisition channel.
    • 

  • Keep values short and readable. Long UTM strings are hard to debug and can get truncated by some platforms.
    • 

  • Always use https. Tagged URLs on http can lose their parameters through redirects or referrer stripping.
    • 

  • Test before you launch. Click the finished URL yourself and confirm that the campaign appears correctly in your analytics dashboard.
    • 






If a tagged URL isn't showing up in your analytics, the most common causes are: a typo in a parameter name (e.g. utm-src instead of utm_source), mixed case across two versions of the same campaign, UTM tags on an internal link, or a redirect that strips the query string. Test with a real click before investing in a campaign.









Common campaign presets


Google Ads


Use utm_source=google, utm_medium=cpc, and a campaign name that matches the one in Google Ads. Add utm_term for the keyword and utm_content to distinguish between responsive search ad variants.


Facebook and Instagram Ads


Use utm_source=facebook or instagram, utm_medium=paid_social, and a descriptive campaign name. Use utm_content to tag different creatives so you can tell which image or video drove the click.


Email newsletters


Use utm_source=newsletter, utm_medium=email, and a campaign name that identifies the specific send. If you A/B test subject lines, add utm_content to differentiate them.


Organic social posts


Use utm_source=twitter (or the platform name), utm_medium=social, and a campaign name that describes the post's purpose. Organic social traffic is often underreported without UTM tags, so this is one of the highest-impact places to add them.


QR codes and offline media


Use utm_source=qr or the name of the printed asset, utm_medium=offline, and a campaign name that identifies the placement. This lets you measure which posters, flyers, or packaging drove traffic.
