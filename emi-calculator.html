What is an EMI Calculator?

An EMI Calculator is a free online tool that calculates the Equated Monthly Installment (EMI) you need to pay every month to repay a loan. EMI stands for Equated Monthly Installment — a fixed amount you pay to a lender each month that includes both principal repayment and interest. The EMI stays the same every month, making it easy to budget for your loan.[reference:0]

This calculator works for any fixed-rate loan — home loans, car loans, personal loans, student loans, business loans, or any other type of installment loan. Whether you're in the US, UK, Europe, India, Australia, Canada, or anywhere else, the underlying EMI formula is the same. You simply enter the loan amount, interest rate, and tenure, and the tool instantly shows your monthly payment, total interest, total cost, and a complete amortization schedule.

Everything runs locally in your browser. Your financial data never leaves your device, there's no signup, and there's no upload. Unlike server-side calculators, this one works entirely offline once the page loads.

The EMI formula explained

EMI is calculated using a standard mathematical formula that accounts for compound interest on the outstanding balance. This is the same formula used by banks and financial institutions worldwide. Here's the formula:

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n / ((1 + r)^n − 1)

Where:

  • P = Principal loan amount (the amount you borrow)
  • r = Monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100)
  • n = Total number of monthly payments (tenure in years × 12, or tenure in months)

Let's walk through a real example. Suppose you borrow ₹10,00,000 at 7.5% annual interest for 20 years:

  • Monthly interest rate (r) = 7.5 ÷ 12 ÷ 100 = 0.00625
  • Number of payments (n) = 20 × 12 = 240
  • EMI = 10,00,000 × 0.00625 × (1.00625)^240 / ((1.00625)^240 − 1)
  • EMI ≈ ₹8,055 per month
  • Total payment = ₹8,055 × 240 = ₹19,33,200
  • Total interest = ₹19,33,200 − ₹10,00,000 = ₹9,33,200

This shows the true cost of borrowing: you pay nearly double the principal over 20 years. This calculator uses the reducing balance method, which is the industry standard and the same method used by most banks globally.[reference:1]

In the early months of a loan, most of your EMI goes towards interest. As the loan progresses, the principal portion grows. This is because interest is calculated on the outstanding balance, which decreases over time. The amortization schedule shows this shift month by month.

What is an amortization schedule?

An amortization schedule is a complete table showing every single payment over the life of your loan. For each month, it shows: the payment number, the opening balance, the principal portion of the EMI, the interest portion, and the closing balance.[reference:2]

Here's what a few rows of an amortization schedule look like for a ₹10,00,000 loan at 7.5% for 20 years:

Sample amortization schedule (first 5 months)

Notice how the interest portion decreases slightly each month while the principal portion increases. This is the essence of amortization. By the end of the loan, the interest portion becomes very small and most of your EMI goes towards principal.

Tips to reduce your EMI

  • Make a larger down payment. A bigger down payment reduces the principal, which directly lowers your EMI. Even a 5% increase in down payment can save significant interest.
  • Choose a longer tenure (carefully). A longer tenure reduces the monthly EMI, but increases total interest. Only choose a longer tenure if affordability is a concern.
  • Improve your credit score. A higher credit score qualifies you for lower interest rates. Even a 0.5% rate reduction can save thousands over the life of a loan.
  • Make prepayments. Any extra payment you make goes directly to the principal, reducing both the outstanding balance and future interest. Most lenders allow prepayment without penalty for floating-rate loans.
  • Compare lenders. Interest rates, processing fees, and prepayment penalties vary widely. Always compare at least 3–4 lenders before signing.
  • Negotiate. Especially for home loans and large personal loans, lenders often have room to negotiate on rates and fees.
  • Consider refinancing. If rates have dropped since you took your loan, refinancing at a lower rate can significantly reduce your EMI. But factor in refinancing costs.

EMI vs total cost — why longer tenure isn't always better

It's tempting to choose the longest possible tenure to minimize the monthly EMI. But longer tenure means more total interest. Here's a comparison for a ₹10,00,000 loan at 7.5% interest:

TenureMonthly EMITotal InterestTotal Payment
10 years₹11,874₹4,24,880₹14,24,880
15 years₹9,272₹6,69,000₹16,69,000
20 years₹8,055₹9,33,200₹19,33,200
30 years₹6,993₹15,17,480₹25,17,480

A 30-year loan has an EMI that's 41% lower than a 10-year loan. But the total interest is more than 3.5 times higher. Always weigh affordability against the total cost of borrowing.
