The five percentage modes explained

1. What is X% of Y?

This is the most common percentage question. You have a percentage and a number, and you want to find the result. For example: "What is 15% of 200?" The answer is 30.

Formula: Result = (X ÷ 100) × Y

When to use it: Calculating discounts ("25% off a $80 item"), tips ("18% of a $50 bill"), tax ("8% sales tax on $120"), or any situation where you know the percentage and the base number.

2. X is what % of Y?

This is the reverse. You have two numbers and you want to know what percentage the first is of the second. For example: "30 is what percent of 200?" The answer is 15%.

Formula: Result = (X ÷ Y) × 100

When to use it: Checking test scores ("I got 85 out of 100 — what's that as a percentage?"), comparing values ("This month's sales are $45,000 out of a $200,000 target"), or understanding proportions.

3. Percentage Change

This calculates the percentage increase or decrease between two values. For example: "From 100 to 125 — what's the percentage change?" The answer is +25%.

Formula: Result = ((New − Old) ÷ |Old|) × 100

When to use it: Tracking price changes, comparing month-over-month sales, measuring growth or decline, or any situation where you need to know how much something has changed relative to where it started.

A positive result means an increase; a negative result means a decrease. The result panel shows this with color coding — green for increases, red for decreases.

4. Increase a number by a percentage

This adds a percentage to a number. For example: "Increase 200 by 15%" gives 230.

Formula: Result = X × (1 + Y ÷ 100)

When to use it: Adding GST or sales tax to a price, applying a markup, calculating a raise, or any situation where you need to add a percentage to a base value.

5. Decrease a number by a percentage

This subtracts a percentage from a number. For example: "Decrease 200 by 15%" gives 170.

Formula: Result = X × (1 − Y ÷ 100)

When to use it: Applying a discount, calculating a sale price, subtracting tax, or any situation where you need to reduce a value by a percentage.

Real-world uses for percentage calculations

Shopping and discounts

A jacket is priced at $120 and it's 30% off. What's the final price? Use the decrease mode: $120 decreased by 30% = $84. You save $36.

You want to double-check that a store's "25% off" sign is being applied correctly. Use the decrease mode on the original price and compare.

GST and sales tax

A service costs $850 and GST is 18%. What's the total? Use the increase mode: $850 increased by 18% = $1,003. The GST amount is $153.

You have a total of $1,180 that includes 18% GST and you want to find the pre-tax price. Use the decrease mode in reverse — or just divide by 1.18. The original was $1,000.

Tips and restaurant bills

A restaurant bill is $64.50 and you want to leave a 20% tip. Use the "X% of Y" mode: 20% of $64.50 = $12.90. The total would be $77.40.

Exam scores and grades

You scored 78 out of 90 on a test. What's your percentage? Use the "X is what % of Y" mode: 78 is 86.67% of 90.

Your final grade is weighted: exams are 60%, coursework is 40%. You got 75% on exams and 88% on coursework. Use the "X% of Y" mode for each component, then add them: (75 × 0.60) + (88 × 0.40) = 45 + 35.2 = 80.2% overall.

Business and finance

Revenue was $48,000 last quarter and $56,000 this quarter. What's the growth rate? Use the percentage change mode: from $48,000 to $56,000 = +16.67%.

A product costs $32 to make and you want a 45% profit margin. What should the selling price be? Use the increase mode: $32 increased by 45% = $46.40.

Health and fitness

You weighed 180 lbs and now weigh 165 lbs. What's the percentage change? Use the change mode: from 180 to 165 = −8.33%, meaning you lost 8.33% of your body weight.

A food label says a serving has 12g of fat and your daily target is 65g. What percentage of your daily value is that? Use the "X is what % of Y" mode: 12 is 18.46% of 65.

Everyday math

Your phone battery is at 47% and you want to know how many hours that is if a full charge lasts 14 hours. Use the "X% of Y" mode: 47% of 14 = 6.58 hours.

A recipe calls for 250g of flour and you want to make 75% of the recipe. Use the "X% of Y" mode: 75% of 250 = 187.5g.

Tips for working with percentages

  • Percentages are reversible for multiplication. 15% of 200 is the same as 200% of 15. Both equal 30. This trick can make mental math easier.
  • "Percent" literally means "per hundred." So 15% is just 15 per 100, or 0.15 as a decimal. Multiplying by 0.15 gives the same result as the percentage formula.
  • Percentage change uses the absolute value of the old number. This handles negative starting values correctly. Going from −10 to +10 is a 200% increase, not a −200% change.
  • Percentage points vs percentage change. If interest rates go from 3% to 4%, that's a 1 percentage point increase but a 33.3% percentage change. Be careful which one you mean.
  • Successive percentages don't add. A 20% discount followed by a 10% discount is not 30% off. It's 28% off, because the second discount applies to the already-reduced price.
  • Use the quick buttons for common rates. GST rates (5%, 12%, 18%, 28%), standard tips (15%, 18%, 20%), and common discounts (10%, 25%, 50%) are all one tap away in the "X% of Y" mode.
