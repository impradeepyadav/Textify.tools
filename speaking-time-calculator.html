How does the Speaking Time Calculator work?

The calculator uses a simple, well‑established formula:

Speaking time (in minutes) = Word count ÷ Words per minute (WPM)

The result is then converted into minutes and seconds for better readability. For example, if you have 300 words and speak at 150 WPM, the speaking time is exactly 2 minutes (300 ÷ 150 = 2). If you speak at 130 WPM, that same text would take about 2 minutes and 18 seconds. We round up the total seconds to the next whole second, so you'll never underestimate the time needed.

This approach is widely used by speech coaches, podcast producers, and event organizers to plan script lengths. Because the calculation is entirely mathematical, the result is objective and repeatable — no guesswork involved.

Using the calculator for timed presentations

When you have a strict time limit, every second counts. Here's how to make the most of the speaking time calculator:

Write your script first

Type your full draft into the calculator. If the estimated time exceeds your slot, trim sentences rather than speaking faster — rushed delivery hurts clarity.

Practice with a timer

Read your script aloud while timing yourself. Compare the actual time to the calculator's estimate. If you're consistently faster or slower, adjust the WPM slider to match your true pace.

Add a buffer

Always leave 10–15% extra time for natural pauses, questions, or technical delays. The calculator gives you the bare speaking time; real‑world delivery always takes a bit longer.
