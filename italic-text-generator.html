What does this Italic Text Generator do?

This free online italic text generator converts ordinary letters and numbers into italic Unicode characters that look slanted without needing any font formatting. Type once, and the tool instantly shows your text in five different italic styles — each one a complete Unicode alphabet you can copy and paste anywhere plain text is accepted.

Unlike a word processor's italic button, which applies formatting that gets stripped when you paste into social media, this tool replaces each character with a different Unicode code point from the Mathematical Alphanumeric Symbols block. That means the italic look is part of the text itself — not a font or a CSS style — so it survives copy-paste into Instagram bios, X (Twitter) display names, Discord usernames, TikTok captions, LinkedIn headlines and WhatsApp messages.

Everything runs locally in your browser. The text you type never leaves your device, there is no signup, and there are no usage limits. Generate as much italic text as you want, as many times as you want.

Where italic text works — and where it doesn't

Because the output is standard Unicode, italic text works almost everywhere plain text is accepted. But a few platforms and use cases need special care.

Works great on

  • Instagram — bios, captions, comments and Notes
  • X (Twitter) — posts, display names and bios
  • Discord — usernames, server nicknames and messages
  • TikTok — bios, captions and comments
  • WhatsApp — messages, group names and your About line
  • Facebook and LinkedIn — posts, bios and profile headlines
  • YouTube — video titles and descriptions

Use with caution

  • Website content and SEO — search engines may not index Unicode italic characters as regular letters. For websites, use standard text with real HTML italic or emphasis tags instead.
  • Resumes and job applications — applicant tracking systems (ATS) often fail to read mathematical alphanumeric characters. Keep styled text out of resumes.
  • Screen readers — assistive tech may read italic Unicode characters letter by letter or by their mathematical names. Use italic for short accents like a name or headline, not for long passages.
  • Older devices — a few older fonts may show blank boxes for the rarer styles like bold italic. The core italic serif and italic sans-serif styles have near-universal support.
