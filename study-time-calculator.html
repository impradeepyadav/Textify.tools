What does this study time calculator do?

This free study time calculator helps you plan a realistic study schedule across multiple subjects. Enter how many hours you want to study for each subject, set how many days you have available and how many hours per day you can actually study, and the tool instantly shows your total study time and a recommended per-day breakdown.

Instead of guessing whether your plan is achievable, you can see at a glance whether the total hours fit within your available time. If the daily requirement exceeds your hours per day, you know you need to trim your plan — or extend your deadline. The visual breakdown also shows what percentage of your total study time goes to each subject, so you can spot imbalances at a glance.

You can add as many subjects as you need. Everything runs in your browser — your schedule is never uploaded anywhere.

How to plan your study schedule

  1. Add your subjects: Click Add Subject and type the subject name and how many hours you want to study for it. Add as many as you need.
  2. Set your available days: Enter how many days you have before your exam or deadline. For a weekly plan, use 7.
  3. Set your hours per day: Enter how many hours per day you can realistically study. Be honest — overestimating here is the fastest way to break your plan.
  4. Click Calculate Schedule: The tool shows total study time, the per-day requirement, and a breakdown per subject.
  5. Adjust and re-run: If the per-day number is too high, reduce subject hours or increase the days available. Re-calculate until the plan fits your real life.

The formula

Per-day study time = Total subject hours ÷ Days available

For example, if you plan 20 total hours across 5 days, you need 4 hours per day. If you only have 2 hours per day, you either need 10 days or you need to cut your total plan down to 10 hours.

Tips for building an effective study plan

  • Be honest about hours per day. Most people can't sustain 8 hours of focused study. 3–4 hours of deep work per day is often more effective than 8 hours of distracted study.
  • Prioritise weak subjects. Give harder subjects a larger share of your hours. Easy subjects can be revised quickly; weak ones need repetition.
  • Build in buffer days. Don't plan the schedule to end the day before the exam. Leave 1–2 buffer days for unexpected delays.
  • Add short breaks. The Pomodoro technique (25 min study, 5 min break) helps maintain focus. Factor breaks into your available hours per day.
  • Re-check your plan every week. Recalculate as you go. If you've fallen behind, adjust hours or extend the deadline before it's too late.
  • Use active recall. Reading is passive. Test yourself, solve problems, and write summaries — that's where real learning happens.
