What does this scientific calculator do?

This free online scientific calculator handles far more than the four basic operations. It supports trigonometry (sin, cos, tan and their inverses), natural and base-10 logarithms, powers and roots, factorials, absolute values, and the mathematical constants π and e. You can switch between degrees and radians, store values in memory, and scroll back through your calculation history.

Unlike a basic calculator, this one evaluates full expressions rather than forcing you to press equals after every operation. Type sin(30) + 2^5 - sqrt(16) and get the answer in one go. The parser respects standard operator precedence, handles nested parentheses, and supports implicit multiplication — so 2π, 3(4+1), and 2sin(30) all work exactly as you'd expect.

Everything runs locally in your browser. Your calculations never leave your device, there's no signup, and there are no usage limits. It's built for students, engineers, teachers, and anyone who needs a reliable calculator without installing an app or opening a spreadsheet.

How to use the scientific calculator

  1. Type your expression. Use the on-screen keypad or your keyboard. You can also type directly into the display box.
  2. Choose DEG or RAD. The DEG/RAD button toggles between degrees and radians for all trigonometric functions. The active mode is shown on the display.
  3. Use 2nd for extra functions. Pressing 2nd swaps the top rows to inverse trig (sin⁻¹, cos⁻¹, tan⁻¹), exponential functions (eˣ, 10ˣ), and additional roots and powers.
  4. Store values in memory. Use M+ to add the current result to memory, M− to subtract, MR to recall it, and MC to clear it. An M indicator lights up when memory holds a value.
  5. Press = to calculate. The result appears instantly below the expression and is added to your history. Use the Ans button to insert the previous result into a new calculation.

The calculator also previews the result live as you type, so you can often see the answer without pressing equals at all.

Tips for using the calculator efficiently

  • Use parentheses generously. sin(30)+2 is unambiguous, but sin(30+2) means something completely different. When in doubt, add brackets.
  • Check your DEG/RAD mode first. The single most common mistake with trig functions is using the wrong angle unit. sin(30) is 0.5 in degrees but −0.988 in radians.
  • Chain with Ans. Instead of retyping a long result, press Ans to drop the previous answer straight into your next expression.
  • Use memory for constants. If you're repeating the same value across several calculations, store it once with M+ and recall it with MR.
  • Let the live preview work for you. The result updates as you type, so you can often skip pressing equals entirely.
  • Click a history entry to reuse it. The history panel isn't just a log — clicking any line loads that expression back into the input.
  • Implicit multiplication is allowed. 2π, 3(4+1), and 2sin(30) all work without an explicit × symbol.
